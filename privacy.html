Privacy policy

Your notes are plain files on your own disk.

BobNote is built so that there is nothing about you for us to collect. This page describes exactly what happens — including the few places where a request has to reach someone else's computer, because saying otherwise would not be true.

Effective October 8, 2026

Your notes

BobNote stores your work as ordinary files in a folder you choose on your own computer. On iPad the library lives inside the app's own storage on the device, and is included in your iPad backup if you keep one. The app does not upload your note contents anywhere. We operate no account system, no sync service and no note storage — there is no server of ours for your notes to be on.

If you put your BobFolder in a location managed by iCloud Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive or backup software, that service moves your files according to its own terms, not ours. The choice, and the control, stay with you.

The app

BobNote sends us no usage analytics, no telemetry and no crash reports. It contains no advertising and no third-party tracking code.

It does communicate with the store you bought it from. When the app confirms what you have unlocked, or restores it on another device, that exchange happens through Apple's App Store services on Mac and iPad, or through the Microsoft Store on Windows, and is governed by that company's privacy policy. We receive from them only the anonymous, aggregated reports every developer gets — never your identity and never your payment details.

Once a day, the Mac app asks this website for a small file that says which version is current, so it can remind you about updates. That request reaches Cloudflare like any other visit to this site, as described below, and carries no account, no note contents and nothing about you beyond what every web request carries.

A few things happen only when you ask for them. If you press play on a YouTube card, the video is loaded from YouTube, which then sees your address and the video you played, under Google's privacy policy; nothing loads until you press play. If you open a link, it opens in your browser, and the app itself makes no request. If, on a Mac, you choose to sign in to OneDrive, you sign in with Microsoft, the app reads from your OneDrive only the files you point it at, and the sign-in stays on your Mac; that exchange is governed by Microsoft's privacy policy, and the feature is off until you turn it on.

The Windows edition

The Windows edition is sold through the Microsoft Store, and anything to do with that purchase is handled by the Store's own services on your PC under Microsoft's privacy policy; there is no BobNote server in that exchange.

Your notes live in a BobNote folder inside your Documents, together with the app's own profile folder on the same PC. Showing a board, a note, a link or a picture makes no network request. The Windows edition does not check for updates on its own — the daily version check described above is made by the Mac app only — and updates arrive through the Microsoft Store.

The Windows edition has no OneDrive sign-in and no sign-in of any kind.

Pressing play on a YouTube card loads the video from YouTube, as described above. Opening a link hands it to your default browser.

This website

This site loads no resources from other origins: no analytics, no advertising, no tracking pixels, no external fonts or scripts. It sets no advertising or analytics cookies.

The site is served by Cloudflare. As a matter of technical necessity they process each request — including your IP address, the page requested and your browser's user-agent — in order to deliver the page and to protect the site from attack, and they may set a strictly necessary security cookie if they challenge traffic they consider suspicious. We do not receive, store or analyse any of this.

If you email us

Writing to support@bobnote.app means we receive your email address, your message and anything you attach. We use it only to answer you. We keep support correspondence for 24 months and then delete it.

If you contact us on Telegram

Writing to us on Telegram means Telegram receives and stores your message under Telegram's own privacy policy, not ours. We see your Telegram username, display name and whatever you send.

The BobNote group is public. Anyone can find it and read it. Your Telegram username and anything you post there are visible to every other member and anyone who looks.

We do not control Telegram and cannot delete what Telegram holds. If you want a private channel, use email.

Neither Telegram channel is required to use BobNote, and neither sends anything to the app.

Your rights

You can ask what personal data we hold about you, ask us to correct it, or ask us to delete it. Write to support@bobnote.app and we will respond within 30 days. In practice the only personal data we are likely to hold or be able to see is a support email you sent us or the Telegram information described above.

Apple on Mac and iPad, or Microsoft on Windows, is the seller, so anything to do with a transaction is held by that store, and that store is the right place to ask.

Children

BobNote is not directed at children. The app collects no personal data from anyone, children included.

Who we are

BobNote is made by Li Donghai, Singapore. Data protection contact: support@bobnote.app.

Changes

If this policy changes we will update this page and change the effective date above.
